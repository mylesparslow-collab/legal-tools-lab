# How we use AI tools

Example Firm LLP (fictional). Statement for clients and proposals.

> DRAFT. Not approved for release.

## What we use

We use five AI-assisted tools in client work. Three are in firmwide use and two are in a limited pilot. Four of five tools in use have a named owner at the firm.

## What we use it for, and what we do not

One of five tools in use is not approved to receive client confidential information.

## How your information is protected

Three of four tools approved for client confidential information do not allow what we enter to be used to train a model. Three of four tools approved for client confidential information are covered by a written agreement with the provider, recorded in our tool registry.

## How work is checked

Four of five tools in use have a written review step: a person at the firm reviews the tool's output before anyone relies on it. We test tools against a fixed set of documents with known answers. Every tool in firmwide use that this test applies to has a passing result on record. Four of five tools in use have a review on record within the past 12 months. We tell you before we use one of these tools to draft part of a court filing in your matter.

## How we bill for AI-assisted work

We bill for the time our lawyers and staff spend, including time spent reviewing and correcting tool output. We do not bill for time a tool saved, and we do not add a separate charge for the tools described here.

## Your terms and opting out

Three of four tools approved for client confidential information have a check against the client's written terms on AI before use on a matter. You can tell us not to use these tools on your matters, or set conditions on their use. Write to your relationship partner or the Office of General Counsel. We record your instruction in the matter intake record and apply it to work done after we confirm receipt.
