LANTERN BAY PORT DISTRICT
Office of the Port Attorney

NOTICE INVITING PROPOSALS
Outside Litigation Counsel: Construction and Environmental Matters
Reference LBPD/LEG/26-118

TO:     Interested law firms
FROM:   Hollis Trevanion, Port Attorney
DATE:   12/01/2026

1.  What the Port District needs.  Lantern Bay Port District (the "Port
District") is looking for one law firm to act as its outside litigation
counsel on construction and environmental disputes for the next four years.
The work is described in Exhibit A. The firm chosen will report to the Port
Attorney and will work with the Port District's Chief Engineer, Desmond
Achterberg, and its Director of Environmental Programs, Yvette Lindqvist-Mora.

2.  Background.  The Port District is a public agency governed by a
five-member Board of Port Commissioners: Chair Leander Voss, Vice Chair Amina
Castellane, and Commissioners Rufus Thackeray, Seren Oyelowo and Beatrix
Halloran. The Executive Director is Gideon Marlowe. Three current matters
will be assigned to the firm at once. The first is a claim by the Port
District's former dredging contractor, Carrowmore Marine Constructors, Inc.,
for extra work and delay on the North Channel deepening project. The second is
a petition filed by the Seagrass Coalition of Lantern Bay challenging the Port
District's approval of the Pier 9 cargo terminal. The third is a dispute with
a tenant, Brindle Wharf Cold Storage LLC, over responsibility for contaminated
soil under its leasehold. Firms should treat all three as adverse to the Port
District.

3.  Term.  The agreement will run for four years from the date it is signed.
Either side may end it on thirty days' notice.

4.  Key dates.  Questions about this notice must be emailed to the Port
Attorney's office by 12/15/2026 at 4:00 PM. Answers to all questions will be
posted on the Port District's website on 12/22/2026. Proposals must be
received no later than 4:30 PM on Friday, 01/15/2027. The Port District
expects to interview short-listed firms on 02/02/2027 and 02/03/2027, and to
bring a recommendation to the Board of Port Commissioners at its meeting of
02/23/2027. There will be no pre-proposal meeting.

5.  Who may propose.  To be considered, a firm must show all of the
following. First, the attorney who will lead the trial team must have been
admitted to practice for no fewer than fifteen (15) years. Second, that
attorney must have tried at least five civil cases to verdict or judgment as
lead trial counsel, at least two of them for a public agency. Third, the firm
must have no fewer than eight (8) attorneys in its litigation group. Fourth,
the firm must not currently represent any party named in paragraph 2 in any
matter. A firm that cannot show all four will not be evaluated.

6.  What to send.  A proposal consists of three things: a cover letter; the
firm's answers to the questionnaire in Attachment B, numbered as the
questions are numbered; and the signed forms listed in Attachment C.

The cover letter must be signed by a partner, must identify the attorney who
will lead the trial team, and must confirm that the firm accepts the insurance
requirements in paragraph 9.

Separately from the questionnaire answers, provide one writing sample: a brief
or motion filed in the last three years on which the proposed lead trial
attorney was the principal author, with client-identifying information
removed if the firm wishes.

7.  How to send it.  Upload the proposal as one PDF file to the Port
District's vendor portal at vendors.lanternbayport.example. The portal closes
at the time stated in paragraph 4 and will not accept late files. Do not send
paper copies, and do not email proposals to Port District staff; proposals
sent in either way will not be opened. The answers to the questionnaire may
not exceed eighteen (18) pages. The cover letter, the writing sample, resumes
and the forms are not counted. Use 11-point type or larger.

8.  How the Port District will choose.  A panel of three Port District
employees will score each proposal. Experience in comparable litigation counts
for 35 points out of 100; the proposed trial team, 30 points; approach and
case management, 15 points; cost, 15 points; and the writing sample, 5
points. Interviews, if held, will not be scored but may be used to decide
between closely ranked firms.

9.  Insurance.  The firm must carry, at its own cost, professional liability
insurance with limits of at least $5,000,000 per claim; commercial general
liability insurance of at least $2,000,000 per occurrence; automobile
liability insurance of at least $1,000,000; and workers' compensation
insurance in the amounts the law requires. The Port District must be named as
an additional insured on the general liability policy.

10. Costs and public records.  The Port District will not reimburse the cost
of preparing a proposal. Proposals are public records once the Board has acted.

11. Reservation of rights.  The Port District may withdraw this notice, extend
any date, reject any proposal, or choose more than one firm.

12. Protests.  A firm that objects to the recommendation must file a written
protest with the Executive Director within seven (7) calendar days after the
recommendation is posted.


EXHIBIT A
SCOPE OF WORK

The firm will defend and prosecute civil actions assigned by the Port
Attorney in state and federal trial courts, on appeal, and in arbitration and
mediation. Assignments are expected to fall into three groups. The first is
construction disputes arising from the Port District's capital program,
including claims for extra work, delay and differing site conditions, and
claims by the Port District for defective work. The second is environmental
litigation, including challenges to project approvals, cost recovery for
contaminated property, and enforcement actions brought by regulators. The
third is related lease and tenant disputes where environmental responsibility
is at issue.

For each assigned matter the firm will prepare an early case assessment and a
litigation budget within forty-five days of assignment, and will update both
every six months. The firm will send the Port Attorney a short written status
report each month, will seek approval before retaining any expert or
consultant, and will not file a dispositive motion or make or respond to a
settlement offer without the Port Attorney's agreement. The firm will attend
closed sessions of the Board of Port Commissioners when asked, usually four
to six times a year.

The Port District does not expect the firm to handle workers' compensation
claims, labor negotiations or routine contract drafting, which are handled by
the Port Attorney's office or by other counsel.


ATTACHMENT B
PROPOSAL QUESTIONNAIRE

Answer every question. Number each answer to match.

B-1.   Describe the firm: when it was founded, how many attorneys it has, where
       its offices are, and how its litigation group is organized.

B-2.   Who would lead the trial team, and who else would be on it? For each
       person give the year of admission, the role proposed, and the trials
       and arbitrations handled in the last ten years.

B-3.   Describe up to six construction disputes the firm has litigated or
       arbitrated for public owners in the last eight years. State the amount
       in dispute and the result.

B-4.   Describe the firm's experience in environmental litigation, including
       challenges to project approvals and cost recovery actions, in the last
       eight years.

B-5.   How would the firm approach the first ninety days of the three matters
       described in paragraph 2 of the notice? What would the early case
       assessment for each contain?

B-6.   How does the firm control litigation cost? Describe the firm's
       budgeting practice, its staffing of depositions and hearings, and how
       it reports to clients.

B-7.   Provide four references from public agency clients for litigation
       handled by the proposed lead trial attorney. Include a contact name,
       telephone number and email address for each.

B-8.   Identify every matter in the last five years in which the firm
       represented, or was adverse to, Carrowmore Marine Constructors, Inc.,
       the Seagrass Coalition of Lantern Bay or Brindle Wharf Cold Storage LLC,
       and any other relationship that could be a conflict of interest.

B-9.   State the hourly rate of each person named in B-2, the rates of
       paralegals, and any discount offered to the Port District. Say whether
       the firm will hold these rates for the first two years.

B-10.  If the firm is chosen, how would it take over the Carrowmore matter
       from the firm now handling it, and how long would that take?

Also tell us:

  *  whether any attorney named in B-2 has been sanctioned by a court in the
     last ten years, and the circumstances;

  *  whether the firm is a certified small business or is owned by a
     resident of Lantern County, and what share of the work would be done from
     an office in Lantern County.
