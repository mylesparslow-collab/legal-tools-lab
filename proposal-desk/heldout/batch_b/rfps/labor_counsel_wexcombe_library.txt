WEXCOMBE REGIONAL LIBRARY DISTRICT

Request for Proposals
Labor and Employment Counsel

Issued by the Human Resources Department
RFP number HR-27-01


This request is written as questions and answers. Please read all of it.


1. WHO IS ASKING, AND FOR WHAT?

Wexcombe Regional Library District (the "Library District") runs eleven branch
libraries and a bookmobile in Wexcombe County. It has about 310 employees.
Most of them are represented by one of two unions: the Wexcombe Library
Workers Guild, which represents librarians and library assistants, and
Service Employees Local 4410, which represents custodial and delivery staff.

The Library District wants to hire one law firm as its labor and employment
counsel for three years. The firm will be the Library District's chief
negotiator in bargaining with both unions, will advise the Human Resources
Department day to day, and will defend the Library District in grievance
arbitrations and before the state labor board.

The Library District is governed by a Board of Library Trustees with five
members: Chair Ottilie Brennagh, Vice Chair Marcus Adeyemi-Holt, and Trustees
Fenella Sorensen, Jacinto Villareal and Winifred Okonkwo-Baines. The Library
Director is Calder Whitlock. The Human Resources Director, Ines Navarrete, is
the only person proposers may contact about this request. Her email address
is hr-rfp@wexcombelibrary.example.


2. WHEN IS EVERYTHING DUE?

The request was published on Thursday, the 10th of December 2026.

If you have questions, send them by email so that they arrive before 5 p.m.
on Monday, the 4th of January 2027. We will send our answers to every firm
that has registered, and post them, on Friday, the 8th of January 2027.

Your proposal has to reach us by 2 p.m. on Thursday, January 14th, 2027. We
do not grant extensions to individual firms.

We plan to interview the top three firms on Tuesday, January 26th, 2027. The
Board of Library Trustees is expected to vote on the contract at its meeting
on Wednesday, February 17th, 2027. Bargaining with the Guild opens in March
2027, so the firm must be ready to start at once.


3. WHAT WILL THE FIRM DO?

Bargaining. Both labor contracts expire on June 30, 2027. The firm will
prepare proposals with the Library Director and the Human Resources Director,
lead the Library District's bargaining team at the table, brief the Board of
Library Trustees in closed session, and draft the final contract language.

Advice. The firm will answer questions from Human Resources on discipline,
leaves of absence, disability accommodation, wage and hour rules,
investigations of complaints, and layoffs. We expect an answer to a routine
question within two business days.

Hearings. The firm will represent the Library District in grievance
arbitrations, disciplinary appeals, unfair practice charges and
unemployment appeals. In the last three years there were nine grievance
arbitrations and two unfair practice charges.

Training. The firm will give two supervisor training sessions a year.

The firm will not handle workers' compensation claims or general liability
claims.


4. WHO CAN APPLY?

You can apply only if all of these are true.

   - Your proposed chief negotiator has practiced law for at least eight
     (8) years.
   - Your proposed chief negotiator has been chief negotiator for a public
     employer in at least six completed negotiations.
   - Your firm has at least four (4) attorneys who practice labor and
     employment law on the employer side.
   - Your firm does not represent any labor union or employee association.
   - Your chief negotiator can attend bargaining sessions in person in
     Wexcombe County, including sessions held in the evening.


5. WHAT GOES IN THE PROPOSAL?

Answer each numbered point below and keep the same numbers.

   #1  Introduction letter. Tell us who you are and who will sign the
       contract for your firm. Keep it to one page.

   #2  About your firm. How big is it, where are its offices, how long has
       it advised public employers, and how many public employers does it
       advise now?

   #3  Your people. Who will be chief negotiator? Who will back that person
       up? Who will handle day-to-day advice and hearings? Give each
       person's background and attach resumes at the end.

   #4  Bargaining record. List the public sector negotiations your proposed
       chief negotiator has led in the last six years. For each one, name
       the employer and the union, and say how long bargaining took and
       whether it went to impasse.

   #5  How you would run our negotiations. Describe how you would prepare,
       how you would work with our bargaining team, how you would keep the
       Board informed, and what you would do if bargaining stalls.

   #6  Hearings and advice. Describe your experience in grievance
       arbitration and labor board proceedings, and how you handle a steady
       flow of small advice questions without running up fees.

   #7  References. Give us four public employer references. At least two
       must be able to speak about your chief negotiator at the bargaining
       table.

   #8  Conflicts. Have you or anyone at your firm ever represented the
       Wexcombe Library Workers Guild, Service Employees Local 4410, or any
       employee of the Library District? Tell us about that and about any
       other possible conflict.

   #9  Price. Give hourly rates for every person named in #3. Offer a fixed
       monthly fee for day-to-day advice if you are willing to. Tell us what
       you would charge for travel and what expenses you would bill.

   #10 Taking over. Our current counsel is retiring. How would you get up
       to speed on our contracts, open grievances and bargaining history
       before March?

Two more things we need from every firm, outside the numbered answers:

   A statement, signed by the proposed chief negotiator, that he or she will
   personally attend every bargaining session unless the Human Resources
   Director agrees otherwise.

   A list of every lawsuit or labor board charge filed against your firm by
   a client in the last five years, or a statement that there are none.


6. WHAT FORMS DO WE HAVE TO FILL IN?

Three forms come with this request. All three must be signed and sent back
with your proposal, or we cannot consider it.

   Form HR-1: Proposal Signature Form
   Form HR-2: Non-Collusion Statement
   Form HR-3: Disclosure of Gifts and Relationships

We also need proof of your professional liability insurance. A copy of the
declarations page is enough.


7. HOW WILL YOU DECIDE?

A panel made up of the Library Director, the Human Resources Director and
one outside member will score the written proposals on these factors:

   Bargaining experience and record ................. 35 percent
   The chief negotiator and team .................... 25 percent
   Plan for our negotiations ........................ 20 percent
   Price ............................................ 15 percent
   References ....................................... 5 percent

Interviews are scored separately, on a pass or fail basis.


8. WHAT INSURANCE DO YOU NEED?

The firm must have professional liability insurance of at least $2,000,000
per claim and general liability insurance of at least $1,000,000 per
occurrence. The firm must also carry workers' compensation insurance for its
own employees. We do not require automobile insurance for this contract.


9. HOW DO WE SEND IT IN?

Email one PDF to hr-rfp@wexcombelibrary.example. Put "Labor Counsel Proposal"
and your firm's name in the subject line. Also mail or deliver three (3)
paper copies to Human Resources, Wexcombe Regional Library District, 15
Reading Room Lane, Wexcombe. The PDF is what counts for the deadline; the
paper copies can follow within three days. We will not accept proposals by
fax.

Your answers to #1 through #10 must fit in fifteen (15) pages. Resumes, the
two extra statements, the forms and the insurance page are not counted. Use
at least 11-point type.


10. ANYTHING ELSE?

We can cancel this request or turn down every proposal. We will not pay you
for preparing a proposal or coming to an interview. Proposals become public
records after the Board votes. If you think we made a mistake in the
process, you have five business days after we announce our choice to tell
the Library Director in writing.
