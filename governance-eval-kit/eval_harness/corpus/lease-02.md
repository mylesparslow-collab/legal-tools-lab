# Office Lease (Full-Service Gross): The Marlinspike Building, Suite 640

_Fictional document for software testing. All parties, people, addresses and facts are invented. Not legal advice and not a form._

THIS OFFICE LEASE (the "Lease") is entered into as of February 9, 2027 (the "Lease Date") by and between CORBEL VANE OFFICE HOLDINGS, LP, a California limited partnership ("Landlord"), and HALVERING STROUD ENGINEERING, INC., a California corporation ("Tenant").

## Background

A. Landlord is the owner of a nine-story multi-tenant office building known as The Marlinspike Building, located at 2200 Ostrander Parkway, Estrella Bend, Caldera Vista County, California, together with its parking structure and grounds.

B. Tenant is a civil and structural engineering firm that employs approximately sixty (60) people. Tenant confirms that it is a for-profit corporation, that it is not a microenterprise, a restaurant or a nonprofit organization, and that it has not given, and is not eligible to give, notice to Landlord that it is a qualified commercial tenant under California law.

C. Tenant wishes to lease part of the sixth floor of the Building for its regional office, and Landlord wishes to lease that space to Tenant, on a full-service gross basis with Tenant paying its share of increases in operating expenses and taxes over a base year.

In consideration of the rents and covenants below, Landlord and Tenant agree as follows.

## Article 4. Base Rent

Section 4.1 Payment. Tenant shall pay Monthly Base Rent in advance on the first day of each month of the Term, in lawful money of the United States, without setoff or deduction except as this Lease expressly permits. Tenant shall pay the first full month's Base Rent that is not abated, $29,049.00, when it signs this Lease.

Section 4.2 Abatement. The abatement in Section 1.8 is an inducement for Tenant to perform this Lease for the whole Term. If Landlord terminates this Lease because of an Event of Default, the unamortized part of the abated Base Rent, amortized on a straight-line basis over the Term, is one of the items that Landlord may include in its damages under Section 19.2(d), to the extent the law allows.

Section 4.3 Additional Rent. All sums that Tenant owes under this Lease other than Base Rent are "Additional Rent". Base Rent and Additional Rent together are "Rent".

Section 4.4 Late Payment. If any installment of Rent is not received by Landlord on its due date, Tenant shall immediately pay a late charge equal to fifteen percent (15%) of the full Monthly Base Rent then in effect, whatever the amount of the installment that is late, and the parties agree that this charge is a fair estimate of Landlord's administrative cost. In addition, every sum not paid when due shall bear interest from its due date until paid at the lesser of ten percent (10%) per annum and the maximum lawful rate (the "Interest Rate"). The late charge and interest are Additional Rent.

Section 4.5 Partial Months. Rent for a partial calendar month is prorated on the basis of a thirty (30) day month.

## Article 5. Operating Expenses and Taxes

Section 5.1 Gross Lease with Base Year. Base Rent includes Tenant's Share of Operating Expenses and Taxes for the Base Year. For each calendar year after the Base Year (a "Comparison Year"), Tenant shall pay as Additional Rent Tenant's Share of the amount, if any, by which Operating Expenses for the Comparison Year exceed Operating Expenses for the Base Year, and Tenant's Share of the amount, if any, by which Taxes for the Comparison Year exceed Taxes for the Base Year. The two calculations are made separately. Tenant is not entitled to a credit if either figure for a Comparison Year is lower than the Base Year figure.

Section 5.2 Operating Expenses. "Operating Expenses" means all costs that Landlord incurs in operating, managing, maintaining, repairing and insuring the Project, computed on an accrual basis under sound real estate accounting practice consistently applied, including:

(a) electricity, gas, water, sewer and other utilities, other than electricity that a tenant pays for separately;

(b) janitorial service, window washing, trash removal and pest control;

(c) maintenance contracts for elevators, mechanical systems, fire and life safety systems and the building automation system;

(d) wages, benefits and payroll taxes of Building staff at or below the level of general manager, equitably allocated if they also work on other properties;

(e) premiums and commercially reasonable deductibles for the insurance Landlord carries on the Project;

(f) a property management fee of three percent (3%) of the gross revenues of the Project;

(g) the fair rental value of an on-site management office of no more than 1,200 rentable square feet;

(h) lobby attendants and access control; and

(i) capital expenditures that are required by a Law first enacted or first made applicable to the Project after the Lease Date, or that are reasonably expected to reduce Operating Expenses, in each case amortized with interest at seven percent (7%) per annum over the useful life of the item, and in the case of a cost-saving item limited each year to the savings reasonably projected.

Section 5.3 Exclusions. Operating Expenses exclude:

(a) capital expenditures other than those allowed by Section 5.2(i);

(b) depreciation, interest and principal on debt, and ground rent;

(c) costs of leasing space, including commissions, allowances, concessions and legal fees;

(d) costs reimbursed by insurance, warranties, condemnation awards or other tenants, other than through operating expense pass-throughs;

(e) costs of services provided to other tenants and not offered to Tenant;

(f) salaries of employees above the level of general manager;

(g) costs to remediate Hazardous Materials that Tenant did not introduce;

(h) fines and penalties, and costs caused by Landlord's negligence or breach of a lease;

(i) charitable and political contributions;

(j) reserves of any kind;

(k) costs of art in the lobby, other than routine cleaning; and

(l) management fees in excess of the fee in Section 5.2(f).

Section 5.4 Gross-Up. If the Building is less than ninety-five percent (95%) occupied during the Base Year or a Comparison Year, Landlord shall adjust the Operating Expenses that vary with occupancy to the amount that would have been incurred had the Building been ninety-five percent (95%) occupied for the whole year. Landlord shall apply the same method to the Base Year and to each Comparison Year.

Section 5.5 Cap on Controllable Expenses. For the purpose of computing Tenant's payments, Controllable Expenses for a Comparison Year shall not exceed Controllable Expenses for the Base Year increased by five percent (5%) per year, compounded and cumulative. "Controllable Expenses" means all Operating Expenses other than utilities, insurance, costs under union contracts, costs of complying with Laws and costs of snow, storm or earthquake response.

Section 5.6 Taxes. "Taxes" means real property taxes and assessments on the Project, business improvement district charges, and any tax on rent or on the business of leasing that takes the place of real property taxes. Taxes exclude Landlord's net income, franchise and estate taxes, and penalties for late payment. If Taxes for the Base Year are later reduced by an appeal, Taxes for the Base Year shall be restated at the reduced amount, and if Taxes for a Comparison Year are later reduced, Landlord shall refund Tenant's Share of the reduction, net of the reasonable cost of the appeal.

Section 5.7 Estimates. Landlord may deliver, before or during each Comparison Year, a good faith estimate of the Additional Rent due under this Article for that year. Tenant shall pay one-twelfth of the estimated amount with each payment of Base Rent. Landlord may revise its estimate no more than twice in a Comparison Year.

Section 5.8 Reconciliation. By May 1 of each year, Landlord shall deliver a statement of the actual Operating Expenses and Taxes for the prior Comparison Year and of Tenant's Share of the increases (the "Reconciliation Statement"). Tenant shall pay any shortfall within thirty (30) days after it receives the Reconciliation Statement, and Landlord shall credit any overpayment against Rent next due, or refund it within thirty (30) days if the Term has ended. Landlord's delay in delivering a Reconciliation Statement is not a waiver, except that Landlord may not bill Tenant for an Operating Expense more than twenty-four (24) months after the end of the year in which the expense was incurred.

Section 5.9 Tenant's Audit. Within one hundred eighty (180) days after it receives a Reconciliation Statement, Tenant may give notice that it will review Landlord's books for the year covered by that statement and for the Base Year. The review shall take place at Landlord's office in the Building or, at Landlord's option, by electronic delivery of records, and shall be made by Tenant's employees or by an accounting firm that is not paid on a contingent fee. If the review shows that Landlord overstated Tenant's payments, Landlord shall refund the overpayment with interest at the Interest Rate. If the overstatement is more than four percent (4%), Landlord shall also pay the reasonable cost of the review, up to $7,500. Tenant shall keep the results confidential, other than disclosure to its advisers and as Laws require.

Section 6.4 Sale of Project. On a sale of the Project, Landlord shall transfer the Security Deposit to the buyer and notify Tenant in writing of the transfer and the buyer's name and address, and Landlord shall then have no further liability for it.

Section 7.1 Use. Tenant shall use the Premises for the Permitted Use only. Section 7.4 Hazardous Materials. Tenant shall not use, store or release on the Project any material that Laws regulate as hazardous, other than office supplies, toner and materials samples in quantities normal for an engineering office and handled in compliance with Laws. Tenant shall indemnify Landlord against loss caused by a release of Hazardous Materials by Tenant or its employees, agents or contractors. Landlord represents that, to the actual knowledge of its general manager, there are no Hazardous Materials in the Premises in violation of Laws as of the Lease Date, and Landlord shall remedy at its cost, and not as an Operating Expense, any such condition that Tenant did not cause.

Section 7.5 Rules. Tenant shall observe the rules in Exhibit C and reasonable additions that Landlord makes on at least thirty (30) days' written notice, if they apply to all office tenants. If a rule conflicts with this Lease, this Lease governs.

Section 8.5 Landlord's Repairs. Landlord shall maintain and repair the Building structure, roof, exterior walls and windows, the Common Areas, and the mechanical, electrical, plumbing, elevator and life safety systems of the Building up to the point they enter the Premises, and shall keep them in the condition of comparable office buildings in the Ostrander Parkway corridor.

Section 8.6 Tenant's Repairs. Tenant shall keep the interior of the Premises, including the Tenant Improvements, its alterations, floor and wall coverings, doors, interior glass, the break room plumbing fixtures and any supplemental cooling unit, in good order and repair, reasonable wear and casualty excepted. Tenant shall pay for repairs to the Building that are needed because of the negligence or misuse of Tenant or its employees or visitors, to the extent that the cost is not covered by the insurance Landlord is required to carry.

Section 8.7 Waiver of Repair Statutes. Tenant waives Civil Code sections 1932(1), 1941 and 1942, and any other law that lets a tenant make repairs at a landlord's cost.

Section 9.3 Liens. Tenant shall not allow a mechanics' lien to be filed against the Project for work it orders. If one is filed, Tenant shall remove it by payment or bond within fifteen (15) business days after Landlord's notice.

All Alterations and the Tenant Improvements belong to Landlord at the end of the Term and shall stay in the Premises, except that Tenant shall remove (a) its cabling above the ceiling and in risers, (b) any supplemental cooling unit and its piping, and (c) any Alteration that Landlord identified for removal in writing when it gave its consent. ## Article 12. Indemnity and Limitation of Liability

Section 12.1 By Tenant. Tenant shall defend, indemnify and hold Landlord and its partners, property manager and lenders harmless from third-party claims for bodily injury or property damage that arise from events in the Premises or from the negligent acts or willful misconduct of Tenant or its employees, agents or contractors anywhere on the Project, except to the extent that Landlord's or its agents' negligence or willful misconduct caused the claim.

Section 12.4 Recourse. Any judgment that Tenant obtains against Landlord shall be satisfied only out of Landlord's interest in the Project and its rents, insurance proceeds and sale proceeds. Landlord's partners and their officers and employees have no personal liability under this Lease.

## Article 13. Casualty

Section 13.1 Landlord's Notice. If fire or another casualty damages the Premises or the Building, Landlord shall deliver within forty-five (45) days a written estimate from its architect of the time needed to restore the damage.

Section 13.2 Restoration or Termination. If the estimate is two hundred seventy (270) days or less from the date of the casualty, Landlord shall restore the Building and the Tenant Improvements with reasonable diligence, and this Lease stays in effect. If the estimate is more than two hundred seventy (270) days, either party may terminate this Lease by notice given within thirty (30) days after the estimate is delivered. Landlord may also terminate if the damage is not covered by the insurance Landlord is required to carry and costs more than $2,000,000 to repair, or if it occurs in the last year of the Term, in each case only if Landlord also terminates the leases of all other tenants similarly affected. Tenant may terminate if the damage occurs in the last year of the Term and will take more than sixty (60) days to restore.

Section 13.3 Rent Abatement. Rent abates from the date of the casualty until restoration is substantially complete, in the proportion that the rentable area Tenant cannot use and does not use bears to the rentable area of the Premises.

Section 13.4 Statutes Waived. Tenant waives Civil Code sections 1932(2) and 1933(4), since this Article states the parties' agreement on casualty.

## Article 14. Eminent Domain

If the whole of the Premises is taken by condemnation, this Lease ends when the condemnor takes possession. If more than fifteen percent (15%) of the Premises, or a part of the Building or parking structure without which Tenant cannot reasonably conduct business, is taken, either party may end this Lease by notice within sixty (60) days after the taking. If this Lease continues, Base Rent and Tenant's Share shall be reduced in proportion to the area taken and Landlord shall restore the rest. Landlord is entitled to the whole award, but Tenant may make a separate claim for moving costs, its personal property and loss of goodwill. Each party waives Code of Civil Procedure section 1265.130.

## Article 15. Assignment and Subletting

Section 15.1 General Rule. Tenant shall not assign this Lease or sublet any part of the Premises (a "Transfer") without Landlord's written consent, which Landlord shall not unreasonably withhold, condition or delay. It is reasonable for Landlord to withhold consent if (a) the proposed transferee's financial condition is not adequate for the obligations it would take on, (b) the proposed use is not allowed by Section 1.11 or would breach an exclusive right granted to another tenant, (c) the proposed transferee is a government agency or a medical clinic, (d) the proposed transferee is an existing tenant of the Building and Landlord has comparable space available for it, or (e) an Event of Default exists.

Section 15.2 Procedure. Tenant shall give Landlord a written request with the name and financial statements of the proposed transferee, the terms of the Transfer and the proposed effective date. Landlord shall respond within fifteen (15) business days. If Landlord does not respond, Tenant may send a second notice that states in capital letters that Landlord's failure to respond within five (5) business days will be deemed consent, and consent is deemed given if Landlord does not respond within that time.

Section 15.3 Recapture. If Tenant proposes to assign this Lease, or to sublet more than seventy-five percent (75%) of the Premises for substantially the rest of the Term, Landlord may, within the fifteen (15) business days in Section 15.2, elect to terminate this Lease as of the proposed effective date. Tenant may withdraw its request within five (5) business days after Landlord's election, in which case the election has no effect.

Section 15.4 Transfer Premium. Tenant shall pay Landlord fifty percent (50%) of all rent and other consideration it receives for a Transfer above the Rent payable under this Lease for the same space, after deducting Tenant's reasonable costs of the Transfer, including commissions, legal fees, improvement costs and free rent.

Section 15.5 Permitted Transfers. Tenant may, without Landlord's consent and without Sections 15.3 and 15.4 applying, assign this Lease or sublet to (a) an entity that controls, is controlled by or is under common control with Tenant, or (b) the successor to Tenant by merger or by purchase of substantially all of Tenant's assets, if the successor has a tangible net worth at least equal to Tenant's on the Lease Date. Tenant shall give notice within ten (10) days after the transaction with reasonable proof that this Section applies.

Section 15.6 Tenant Remains Liable. No Transfer releases Tenant. Landlord's consent to one Transfer does not waive the need for consent to another. Tenant shall pay Landlord's reasonable legal fees for reviewing a request, up to $2,500.

Section 16.3 Landlord's Certificate. Landlord shall deliver a similar certificate to Tenant, a permitted transferee or Tenant's lender within fifteen (15) business days after Tenant's request, no more than twice a year.

## Article 19. Default and Remedies

Section 19.1 Events of Default. An "Event of Default" occurs if:

(a) Tenant does not pay Rent within five (5) business days after Landlord gives written notice that the payment is past due;

(b) Tenant does not deliver a subordination agreement within the time in Section 16.1, or does not keep in force the insurance Article 11 requires, and the failure continues for five (5) business days after written notice;

(c) Tenant breaches any other term of this Lease and does not cure within thirty (30) days after written notice, or within the longer time, up to one hundred twenty (120) days, reasonably needed to cure a breach that cannot be cured in thirty (30) days, if Tenant starts within thirty (30) days and works diligently;

(d) Tenant abandons the Premises within the meaning of Civil Code section 1951.3; or

(e) Tenant files a voluntary petition in bankruptcy or makes an assignment for the benefit of creditors, or an involuntary petition is filed against Tenant and remains undismissed for ninety (90) days.

The notices in this Section are in lieu of, and not in addition to, notices under Code of Civil Procedure section 1161, if served in the manner that section requires.

Section 19.2 Termination Damages. If an Event of Default occurs, Landlord may end Tenant's right of possession by written notice and recover from Tenant, under Civil Code section 1951.2:

(a) the worth at the time of award of unpaid Rent that had been earned when this Lease ended;

(b) the worth at the time of award of the amount by which the unpaid Rent that would have been earned from then until the time of award exceeds the rental loss that Tenant proves could have been reasonably avoided;

(c) the worth at the time of award of the amount by which the unpaid Rent for the rest of the Term after the award exceeds the rental loss that Tenant proves could be reasonably avoided; and

(d) any other amount needed to compensate Landlord for the detriment that Tenant's breach proximately caused, or that would be likely to result from it in the ordinary course.

For clauses (a) and (b), worth at the time of award is computed with interest at the Interest Rate. For clause (c), it is computed by discounting at the discount rate of the Federal Reserve Bank of San Francisco at the time of award plus one percent (1%).

Section 19.3 Continuing the Lease. Landlord shall have the remedy described in Civil Code section 1951.4, under which a lessor may continue a lease in effect after the lessee's breach and abandonment and recover rent as it becomes due if the lessee has the right to sublet or assign subject only to reasonable limitations. If Tenant breaches this Lease and abandons the Premises, Landlord may therefore leave this Lease in force, and collect each installment of Rent as it falls due, for as long as Landlord does not terminate Tenant's right to possession. From the date of any Event of Default and for so long as Landlord is exercising its rights under this Section 19.3, Article 15 shall not apply, Tenant shall have no right to assign this Lease or sublet the Premises, and Landlord may refuse any proposed assignee or subtenant without regard to whether the refusal is reasonable.

Section 19.4 Cure by Landlord. If Tenant does not perform an obligation within the cure period that applies, Landlord may perform it for Tenant's account, and Tenant shall repay Landlord's reasonable cost with interest at the Interest Rate within thirty (30) days after Landlord's invoice.

Section 19.5 Default by Landlord. Landlord is in default if it does not perform an obligation within thirty (30) days after Tenant's written notice, or within the longer period reasonably needed if Landlord starts within thirty (30) days and works diligently. Tenant shall give any lender whose address it has been given a copy of the notice and the same time to cure. Tenant's remedies for Landlord's default are an action for damages or specific performance. Tenant may not withhold Rent, except as a final judgment allows.

Section 19.6 Remedies Not Exclusive. The remedies in this Article are in addition to all others that the law gives the parties.
