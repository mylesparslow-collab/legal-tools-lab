# PUBLIC WORKS CONSTRUCTION CONTRACT: LARKMOOR COMMUNITY CENTER RENOVATION AND SEISMIC UPGRADE

**City Project No. CIP 26-208**

THIS PUBLIC WORKS CONSTRUCTION CONTRACT (this "Contract") is made and entered into on April 14, 2026, by and between the **CITY OF MARROW GLEN**, a California general law city and municipal corporation ("City"), whose City Hall is located at 40 Tolland Square, Marrow Glen, California, and **OXBOW & FENN BUILDERS, INC.**, a California corporation ("Contractor"), whose office is located at 1720 Cinderpath Avenue, Suite 3, Hale Crossing, California.

## BACKGROUND

1. City owns the Larkmoor Community Center, a two-story, 31,400 square foot building constructed in 1962 at 315 Pippin Orchard Way, Marrow Glen (the "Center"). The Center houses the City's senior nutrition program, a preschool wing, a gymnasium and public meeting rooms.

2. City is a local agency. The renovation of the Center is a public project paid for from City's general capital fund, proceeds of the Measure R parks and facilities sales tax, and a grant from the Halvering County Community Facilities Program. No private funds are being used.

3. On February 3, 2026 the City Council approved the plans and specifications prepared by Thistledown Architecture Studio ("Architect") and authorized the City Clerk to advertise for bids. City received five sealed bids, which were opened publicly on March 12, 2026.

4. On April 7, 2026 the City Council adopted Resolution No. 26-031 awarding this Contract to Contractor as the lowest responsible bidder submitting a responsive bid, in the amount of Eight Million Four Hundred Fifteen Thousand Dollars ($8,415,000).

5. Contractor holds a Class B General Building Contractor license (No. 000862) and is registered as a public works contractor with the Department of Industrial Relations (No. PW-LR-0000862).

The parties therefore agree as follows.

## ARTICLE 2. THE WORK

**2.1 Performance.** Contractor shall provide all labor, materials, equipment, tools, utilities, transportation and supervision required to complete the Work. The principal components of the Work are:

(a) abatement of asbestos-containing floor tile, mastic and pipe insulation, and stabilization of lead-based paint, in accordance with the hazardous materials survey dated October 2025 included in the Project Manual;

(b) seismic strengthening, including new concrete shear walls at grid lines 2 and 9, steel collectors at the roof diaphragm, and new spread footings and grade beams requiring excavation to a depth of approximately seven (7) feet below the existing slab;

(c) replacement of the roofing system and installation of a 96-kilowatt rooftop photovoltaic array;

(d) replacement of the heating, ventilating and air conditioning system with an all-electric variable refrigerant flow system;

(e) a new commercial kitchen serving the senior nutrition program;

(f) accessibility upgrades, including a new elevator, eight renovated restrooms, new entry ramps and an accessible path of travel from the parking lot;

(g) new fire sprinkler and fire alarm systems throughout the Center;

(h) interior finishes, casework, gymnasium flooring and audiovisual systems; and

(i) site work, including replacement of the sewer lateral, new storm drain connections, parking lot resurfacing and landscape planting.

**2.2 Additive Alternate.** City accepted Additive Alternate No. 1 (operable partition and acoustical treatment at the multipurpose room) in the amount of $185,000, which is included in the Contract Sum.

**2.3 Contractor's Representations.** Contractor represents that it has examined the Center and the Contract Documents and is satisfied as to the nature and location of the Work and the general and local conditions that can affect it, to the extent those conditions are reasonably ascertainable from a careful pre-bid inspection.

**2.4 Supervision.** Contractor shall keep a competent superintendent, Lucan Avery-Stroud, at the Center whenever Work is in progress. The superintendent represents Contractor, and directions given to the superintendent are binding on Contractor. Contractor's project manager is Mireille Tansley.

**2.5 Permits.** City will issue the building permit at no charge to Contractor. Contractor shall obtain and pay for all other permits, including the air district notification for asbestos removal, the elevator permit to operate, and any encroachment permit for work in Pippin Orchard Way.

**2.6 Use of the Site.** The Center will be vacated by City before the Notice to Proceed, except for the detached preschool annex, which will remain in operation. Contractor shall maintain a fenced, safe and separate path of travel to the annex at all times, shall not permit construction traffic in the annex drop-off zone between 7:30 a.m. and 9:00 a.m. or between 2:30 p.m. and 4:00 p.m. on school days, and shall require every worker on site to wear visible identification. Contractor shall comply with City's requirements for fingerprinting or continuous supervision of workers who may have contact with children.

**2.7 Submittals.** Contractor shall submit a submittal schedule within twenty-one (21) Days after the Notice to Proceed and shall allow fourteen (14) Days for the Architect's review of each submittal. Review of submittals is for general conformance with the design concept and does not relieve Contractor of responsibility for errors, quantities or dimensions.

**3.6 Compensable Delay.** If a delay for which City is responsible is unreasonable under the circumstances involved and was not within the contemplation of the parties, Contractor is entitled, in addition to a time extension, to recover its actual, reasonable and documented field and jobsite costs resulting from the delay, plus home office overhead at the daily rate of $1,150. **5.5 Amounts City May Withhold.** City may withhold from a progress payment, in addition to retention: (a) the amount of any stop payment notice, plus twenty-five percent (25%), unless Contractor posts a release bond; (b) accrued liquidated damages; (c) the reasonable cost to correct defective Work that Contractor has failed to correct after notice; (d) amounts the Labor Commissioner directs City to withhold; and (e) amounts City has paid on Contractor's behalf. ## ARTICLE 12. CLAIMS AND DISPUTE RESOLUTION

**12.1 Definition.** A "Claim" under this Article is a separate demand by Contractor, sent by registered or certified mail with return receipt requested, for (a) a time extension, including relief from liquidated damages; (b) payment of money or damages arising from Work done by or on behalf of Contractor to which Contractor is not otherwise expressly entitled; or (c) payment of an amount that City disputes.

**12.2 Statutory Procedure.** Claims are resolved under Public Contract Code section 9204, summarized here. Contractor shall furnish reasonable documentation to support each Claim. City shall review the Claim and, within forty-five (45) Days after receipt (or a longer period the parties agree to, or within three (3) Days after the next duly noticed City Council meeting following that period if Council approval is needed), provide a written statement identifying what portion is disputed and what portion is undisputed. City shall pay any undisputed portion within sixty (60) Days after issuing its statement. If Contractor disputes City's statement, or City does not respond in time, Contractor may demand in writing an informal meet and confer conference, which City shall schedule within thirty (30) Days.
