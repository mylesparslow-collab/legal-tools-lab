## Left out of the report

- Unusual circumstances for the 14-day extension now include a cyberattack and a Governor-proclaimed
  state of emergency (AB 370, Stats. 2025, ch. 34, effective 1/1/2026). § 7922.535(c)(5)-(6).
- SB 707 added remote public access duties for "eligible legislative bodies", including a disruption
  policy due by 7/1/2026. The section was amended again by SB 1187 (Stats. 2026, ch. 324, effective
  9/18/2026) and is repealed 1/1/2030. § 54953.4. Not modeled here.
- Special meetings cannot take up pay of the legislative body or a local agency executive, except in a
  budget discussion. § 54956(b).
- A written denial must name the people responsible for it. § 7922.540(b).

## Matches the statute text

- 72-hour regular agenda posting (§ 54954.2(a)(1)) and 24-hour special meeting notice and posting (§ 54956(a)).
- Remote participation as a disability accommodation is treated as in-person attendance for all
  purposes including quorum-location rules; the member discloses adults in the room; the posted-location
  rules of § 54953(b) do not apply (§ 54953(c)). The text also requires audio and video unless a
  disability-related condition requires off-camera participation.
- The social media rule from AB 992 remains in § 54952.2(b)(3) as amended by SB 707, with no repeal date.
- § 54956.8 (real property negotiators), § 54956.9(d) (litigation), § 54957.6 (labor negotiators) are the
  right closed-session authorities and each has a § 54954.5 form.
- 14-day cap on the unusual-circumstances extension (§ 7922.535(b)).
- §§ 7927.705, 7928.705 and 7929.210 exist and cover other-law/privilege exemptions, real estate
  appraisals, and information security records.
