Planning figures are the ones to price from. They are built to hold up on the next matter: a quantile position that allows for the small sample, then raised (never lowered) by how far earlier matters of this type ran over their own bands. Of the comps (descriptive): the interpolated quantile of the 41 comps. It describes these comps; in the back-test it understated overruns. Its interval is a bootstrap interval (1000 resamples, seed 20260) showing how much the figure would move with a different draw of 41 matters; it is not a forecast range.

Read the planning columns first. They place the fee among the planning figures and give the walk-forward back-test for this matter type at the bands either side: each past matter judged against bands set only from what was known the day it opened. The in-sample columns count the same comps the figures were built from, so they flatter the fee.

Margin at a planning figure: revenue under the scenario if the work came to that figure at standard rates, less cost taken as 32.5% of that figure (the comps' total cost over their total standard value). A phased fee has no single figure to compare and is judged in sample only. Cost uses the cost rates in the input file: PLACEHOLDER values for the demo only. Internal cost per hour by level is an input that finance must supply; these figures are invented and are not estimates of any firm's costs.

  • Fixed fee $100,000: Revenue is the fee on every comp, whatever the work turned out to be.
  • Capped fee, cap $130,000: Billed hourly at standard rates up to the cap; work above the cap is not paid.

Fee for a target chance of staying within the fee

Target 80%. Planning figure: a fixed fee of $199,500 to cover the work at standard rates; $64,900 to cover cost at the input cost rates.

How it was made: the comp at position ceil((n+1) x 0.80) ($128,300), raised x1.41 from 31 past outcomes of this type; that came out below a planning band for a lower target, so the band is used instead. The cost figure is the first one times the comps' cost share of standard value.

In sample, for comparison: $127,500 would have covered the standard-rate value on 33 of 41 (80%) comps and $41,300 would have covered cost on 33 of 41 (80%) comps (observed comp amounts, n=41). Past shares are not probabilities for the next matter. Out of sample: At the planning P75, 10 of 31 back-tested matters cost more; the band implies about 8. Scope assumptions and exclusions from the comp evidence

  • The 10 comps above the P75 cost $122,100 more on average than the other 31. Discovery and diligence accounts for 51% of that gap ($61,900). Set a scope limit or a separate fee for that phase.
  • 4 matters of this type closed abnormally (settled early, transferred or other) and are not in the comps. The figures assume the matter runs to completion; say what happens to the fee if it ends early.
  • The phase split for 5 of 41 comps comes from the date-window fallback, not task codes. Treat per-phase figures as lower confidence; totals are not affected.
  • Across the 41 comps, 5.3% of worked value was written down before billing and 3.8% of invoiced fees was not collected. A fee built from billed amounts would understate the work; the figures here use worked hours.
  • Costs for this matter type appear to have risen: the 26 matters opened from 2022-04-12 cost 1.78x the 15 opened before, at the same rates (medians $96,800 and $54,300).   • Matters that closed abnormally (settled early, transferred, other) are excluded.
