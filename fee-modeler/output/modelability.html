Back-test by matter type: planning bands, with the plain comp quantiles beside them

Walk-forward. Each matter is modeled only from same-type matters that closed before it opened, using time dated before it opened; the change check and the band adjustment are also run as of that day; where the check fires, each band is the higher of the recent-only and whole-history versions. Abnormally closed matters are neither tested nor used as comps. Amounts are at today's standard rates on both sides.

Planning bands: the comp at position ceil((n + 1) x q) among the comps, raised by a factor learned from how earlier matters of the type came in against their own bands (never lowered; needs 12 earlier outcomes, otherwise unadjusted). Comp quantiles (descriptive): interpolated quantiles of all earlier closed matters of the type; they describe the comps and are shown for comparison.

Upper bands are judged one-sided with an exact binomial check: a band is said to understate overruns when seeing that many matters above it, or more, would happen less than 1 time in 20 if the band were right. With about a dozen matter types and two bands each, expect the odd flag by chance.

Change check: matters of a type are put in order of opening and every split with at least 8 on each side is tried. A type is flagged when no more than 1 in 20 of 1000 seeded reshuffles of the same costs split as cleanly as the best real split, and the medians either side differ by 1.25x or more. The date of the change is estimated, not supplied. It is a prompt to look: it misses real changes and flags types where nothing changed (measured rates are in the README). These thresholds are settings of this tool, not industry figures.
