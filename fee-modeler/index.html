Fee modeler

Evidence for fixed and capped fees from past matters of the same type

Personal portfolio demo. All data is synthetic: every client, matter, timekeeper and rate is invented. Not financial, pricing or legal advice. Never run on real matter data from this page. The tool shows evidence and risk; a person decides the price.

For a proposed matter, the tool picks comparable closed matters, values their hours at today's rates, shows the spread of what they cost, and tests fee structures against that spread. It refuses to give a figure when there are too few comps, and the back-test below says how well its bands have held up.

1. Does it work on this data? Walk-forward back-test

Walk-forward. Each matter is modeled only from same-type matters that closed before it opened, using time dated before it opened; the change check and the band adjustment are also run as of that day; where the check fires, each band is the higher of the recent-only and whole-history versions. Abnormally closed matters are neither tested nor used as comps. Amounts are at today's standard rates on both sides.

Planning bands: the comp at position ceil((n + 1) x q) among the comps, raised by a factor learned from how earlier matters of the type came in against their own bands (never lowered; needs 12 earlier outcomes, otherwise unadjusted). Comp quantiles (descriptive): interpolated quantiles of all earlier closed matters of the type; they describe the comps and are shown for comparison.

Upper bands are judged one-sided with an exact binomial check: a band is said to understate overruns when seeing that many matters above it, or more, would happen less than 1 time in 20 if the band were right. With about a dozen matter types and two bands each, expect the odd flag by chance.

Change check: matters of a type are put in order of opening and every split with at least 8 on each side is tried. A type is flagged when no more than 1 in 20 of 1000 seeded reshuffles of the same costs split as cleanly as the best real split, and the medians either side differ by 1.25x or more. The date of the change is estimated, not supplied. It is a prompt to look: it misses real changes and flags types where nothing changed (measured rates are in the README). These thresholds are settings of this tool, not industry figures.

More overruns than a band implies is the direction that loses money on a fixed or capped fee, so it is the one judged. The planning bands are the fix for the shortfall the plain comp quantiles show in the right-hand columns; they are wider as a result. Measured on other generated firms: calibration study, drift study.

One dot per comp, placed by cost at today's standard rates; lighter dots are over the fee. Hover a dot for the matter. The explorer recomputes in your browser from the comp amounts in whole cents. The memos are the figures of record.

5. Run it

python run_demo.py                 # regenerate data, outputs and this page
python -m feemodel profile data/export_b --mapping data/mappings/export_b.json
python -m feemodel backtest
python -m feemodel comps --proposal data/proposals/P-001.json
python -m feemodel price --proposal data/proposals/P-001.json
python -m feemodel calibration-study   # planning bands on 20 other generated firms
python -m feemodel drift-sensitivity   # what the change check catches, old rule beside new
python -m pytest -q

To try it on real data later, read SWAP_IN_REAL_DATA.md first. The order is: profile, then back-test, then price.
