Fee modeler

Evidence for fixed and capped fees from past matters of the same type

Personal portfolio demo. All data is synthetic: every client, matter, timekeeper and rate is invented. Not financial, pricing or legal advice. Never run on real matter data from this page. The tool shows evidence and risk; a person decides the price.

For a proposed matter, the tool picks comparable closed matters, values their hours at today's rates, shows the spread of what they cost, and tests fee structures against that spread. It refuses to give a figure when there are too few comps, and the back-test below says how well its bands have held up.

Upper bands are judged one-sided with an exact binomial check: a band is said to understate overruns when seeing that many matters above it, or more, would happen less than 1 time in 20 if the band were right. With about a dozen matter types and two bands each, expect the odd flag by chance.

More overruns than a band implies is the direction that loses money on a fixed or capped fee, so it is the one judged. Some shortfall is expected when bands come from a few dozen matters or fewer: quantiles of a small sample are narrower than the spread of the next matter.

One dot per comp, placed by cost at today's standard rates; lighter dots are over the fee. Hover a dot for the matter. The explorer recomputes in your browser from the comp amounts in whole cents. The memos are the figures of record.

5. Run it

python run_demo.py                 # regenerate data, outputs and this page
python -m feemodel profile data/export_b --mapping data/mappings/export_b.json
python -m feemodel backtest
python -m feemodel comps --proposal data/proposals/P-001.json
python -m feemodel price --proposal data/proposals/P-001.json
python -m pytest -q

To try it on real data later, read SWAP_IN_REAL_DATA.md first. The order is: profile, then back-test, then price.
