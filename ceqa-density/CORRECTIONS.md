# Corrections to the research report (Tool 10)

The report's Tool 10 entry was checked against statute and rule text on November 25, 2026.
The California Legislature's site (leginfo) could not be reached from the build environment, so
statutes were read on copies of the chaptered bill text (LegiScan) and a code mirror (california.public.law).
The Rules of Court were read on courts.ca.gov. Each number's source and status is in `legal_data.json`.

| # | Report says | Text says | Cite |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Agencies have 60 days "from service" to certify the record. | 60 days from the date the *request to prepare the record* is served on the agency. The petitioner must serve the request and petition within 10 business days of filing. The court holds a case management conference within 30 days of filing. | PRC 21167.6(a), (b)(1) |
| 2 | Says nothing about extensions. | Time is extended only by stipulation of all served parties or court order. No single extension over 60 days unless the court finds it is in the public interest. | PRC 21167.6(c) |
| 3 | SB 131 excludes internal emails on "qualifying housing/infrastructure projects". | The narrower record rule applies to *every* project that does not include a distribution center or oil and gas infrastructure. It is not limited to housing. | PRC 21167.6(e)(10)(B)(ii) |
| 4 | Excluded material comes back in if "reviewed by designated senior executives". | The exception covers material consulted or reviewed by the lead agency executive, a local agency executive (Gov. Code 3511.1(d)), or another administrative official in a supervisory role who is reviewing the project. | PRC 21167.6(e)(10)(B)(iii) |
| 5 | The exclusion covers "internal agency staff emails, notes". | The carve-out in (B)(iii) is for *electronic* internal communications, including emails, not presented to the final decision-making body. Separately, (B)(i) drops the words "staff notes" that (A)(i) keeps. The agency may still include other documents. Paper memoranda stay in. | PRC 21167.6(e)(10)(B) |
| 6 | "Heavy distribution centers" keep the old rule, citing *Golden Door Properties*. | The carve-out is statutory, not from the case: a warehouse distribution center (Lab. Code 2100) of 50,000 sq ft or more, or oil and gas infrastructure. | PRC 21167.6(e)(10)(A)(ii), 21060.4, 21064.8 |
| 7 | SB 131 excludes "unpublished draft environmental documents". | (e)(10) lists drafts "released for public review". Whether that wording is new in SB 131 was not confirmed; the tool sends unreleased drafts to attorney review instead of excluding them. | PRC 21167.6(e)(10) |
| 8 | AB 130 exemption: up to 5 acres for builder's remedy projects. | 4 acres. A law firm summary says SB 158 lowered it from 5 to 4. | PRC 21080.66(a)(1)(B) |
| 9 | Tribal consultation clocks come from SB 158. | The clocks are in 21080.66(b): notice in 14 days, 60 days to respond, start within 14 days of acceptance, conclude in 45 days plus one 15-day extension. A 30-day approval clock attributed to SB 158 was not found in the text read. | PRC 21080.66(b) |
| 10 | SB 4 land "owned prior to Jan. 1, 2024". | "On or before January 1, 2024." | Gov. Code 65913.16(c)(1) |
| 11 | SB 4 stacks with the AB 1287 additional bonus for 100% total. | It does not appear to. SB 4 requires a 100% affordable project. The additional bonus is limited to (b)(1)(A), (B), (D) projects and is barred when more than 50% of total units are restricted. An SB 4 project gets the 100%-affordable bonus instead: 80% of lower income units, or no density cap within half a mile of a major transit stop. Attorney to confirm. | Gov. Code 65913.16(c)(9), (j); 65915(v)(1), (f)(3)(D) |
| 12 | Additional bonus is "up to 50% for Very Low / Moderate units". | Moderate tops out at 50% (15% of units). Very low tops out at 38.75% (10% of units). The base project must already be at the top tier (24% lower, 15% very low, or 44% moderate). | Gov. Code 65915(v)(1), (v)(2) |
| 13 | Not mentioned. | The 50% cap means a 44% moderate project can add only about 5-6% more restricted units. A 100% total bonus needs a lower-share base, such as 15% very low plus 15% moderate. | Gov. Code 65915(v)(1) |
| 14 | "1-4+ concessions". | 1 to 4 by tier (4 needs 16% very low or 45% moderate), and 5 for 100% affordable projects. Senior-only projects get none. | Gov. Code 65915(d)(2) |
| 15 | "0-parking minimums within 1/2 mile of major transit (65915(p) / AB 2097)". | Under 65915(p), zero parking is only for 100% affordable projects near transit. Other qualifying projects near transit get 0.5 spaces per unit (20% lower or 11% very low) or 0.5 per bedroom (40% moderate). AB 2097 is a separate statute, Gov. Code 65863.2, and is not cited in (p). | Gov. Code 65915(p)(2), (p)(3) |
| 16 | "Unlimited" development-standard waivers. | No numeric cap, but waivers are denied for unmitigable health or safety impacts, historic resource impacts, or conflict with state or federal law, and are limited for unlimited-density projects. SB 4 bars using a concession or waiver for more height in non-residential zones. | Gov. Code 65915(e); 65913.16(j)(2)(B) |
| 17 | Rule 3.2205 requires "hyperlinked electronic indices and Bates-numbered PDF volumes". | Rule 3.2205 sets the order of documents and requires a detailed index. Electronic format is rule 3.2207: bookmarked, text-searchable files; the index *may* include hyperlinks. Consecutive page numbers and the 300-page bound volume limit are in rule 3.2208 (paper format). | Cal. Rules of Court 3.2205, 3.2207, 3.2208 |

Confirmed as stated: ceiling rounding of each component (65915(q), (f)(5)); 11 record categories in 21167.6(e)(1)-(11);
SB 131 is Stats. 2025, ch. 24, effective June 30, 2025; the 20-acre general limit in 21080.66.
