# Facilitator guide

For the person running a catch-the-error session before a group is given access to AI drafting or review features.

This is a personal portfolio demo. The documents are fictional, the errors in them were planted by hand, and nothing here is legal advice or was reviewed by an attorney. The statute sections behind the planted errors were read by an AI assistant on November 25, 2026. Have an attorney confirm them before running this with a real group.

## Before the session

- Open `index.html` on the machine each person will use, or share the folder. Nothing is installed and nothing is sent anywhere.
- Pick the practice area closest to the group's work, or Mixed. Pick rotation A the first time; B or C for a group that has done this area before.
- Open `facilitator.html` on your own machine.
- Each person needs a laptop or a tablet. A phone is too small for this.
- Decide how result files reach you (a shared folder, email, a USB stick). The files hold marks and counts and no names.

## A 30 minute session

Use the **20 minute** set. The exercises take about 16 minutes; the rest of the half hour is yours.

| Minute | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 0 to 3 | Say what this is: three short exercises on fictional drafts with planted errors. Say what happens to the results: saved without names, added up for the group, no individual scores shown. Show how to mark (select text, choose a type; or arrow keys and 1, 2, 3). |
| 3 to 9 | **Timed spot check.** Five minutes on a whole agreement, then a minute on the answer screen. Do not discuss yet. |
| 9 to 13 | **Which would you verify?** About three minutes, then the answer screen. |
| 13 to 23 | **Review an AI draft.** An excerpt of about 1,300 words with no clock; suggest eight or nine minutes. Then the answer screen. |
| 23 to 25 | Everyone opens the session summary, saves the JSON file and gets it to you. Load the files on the facilitator page. |
| 25 to 30 | Debrief from the facilitator page, on a shared screen. |

Shorter and longer versions:

- **15 minutes:** the 10 minute set (spot check and verify list), two minutes of introduction, three of debrief.
- **45 minutes:** the 30 minute set (the review uses an excerpt of about 2,500 words), with ten minutes of debrief.

The minutes for each exercise are planning figures. They assume careful reading at about 150 words a minute and have not been tried on a group.

## The three points for the debrief

Each one comes from a number or a screen the exercise produces. Use the group's own results; if the results do not show the point, say so and do not force it.

**1. Skimming misses things.** The "By exercise" table on the facilitator page gives the share of planted errors the group found in the five-minute spot check and in the untimed review. Read out both. Then open the first entries in the debrief list that came from the spot check and ask where in the document they were. The planted errors do not announce themselves: each is a clause that reads like the ones around it, with a number or a condition that conflicts with a statute.

**2. Confident wording is not evidence.** In the verify list, all ten statements came from the same draft in the same voice, and five were planted errors. Ask who left a planted statement unticked and what made it look safe. The answer screen says it directly: the wording does not tell you which is which. The only way to know was to check the statement against the source.

**3. A mark is a question, not an answer.** Look at three things together: errors that were found but given the wrong type, false alarms, and the list of unplanted paragraphs that two or more people marked. Finding the right place is not the same as knowing what is wrong with it, and a confident flag on a clause that was not planted costs someone time to run down. Be careful here: the answer key covers planted errors only. A false alarm in this exercise means "not in the key", not "the clause is fine". The step that settles it is the same as in point 2: read the rule.

Close with the working rule the three points add up to: read the whole draft, check what you rely on against the source, and treat your own flags and the tool's flags the same way.

## What the exercise does not teach

- **The law.** Each answer shows a short explanation and the rule as it was read for the catalog. That is enough to see why a clause was planted. It is not training in landlord-tenant, health facility, public contract or employment law, and it has not been reviewed by an attorney.
- **How AI tools actually fail.** These errors were planted by hand in synthetic contracts. They are all conflicts between a clause and a statute. Invented citations, misread facts, dropped paragraphs, summaries that leave out the bad part and answers that change when asked twice are not in here.
- **How often a tool is wrong.** Half the verify statements and six to nine clauses in each agreement are planted. Real drafts will not have that rate, in either direction.
- **How to use any particular product,** how to write a prompt, or what may be put into a tool. Confidentiality, client consent and outside counsel guidelines need their own session.
- **Anything about an individual.** One sitting, on fictional documents outside a person's practice area, with a clock running, says little about that person. The page shows group results only for that reason. Do not rebuild individual scores from the files.
- **Missing clauses.** The exercise is built on marking text, so it is weak on what is not there. One planted omission cannot be marked at all and is shown without being scored.

## If something goes wrong

- A result file is skipped as "made with a different build": that person's copy of the app is older or newer than yours. Use the same folder for everyone.
- Someone reloads the page mid-exercise: the session starts over. Have them run the remaining exercises one at a time from "Or run a single exercise".
- Storage is blocked in the browser: everything still works, except reopening the last summary after leaving it. Save the JSON before closing the tab.
